# Quality assurance

## What quality means here

A strong lesson produces observable capability. Learners should build something, test it under imperfect conditions, record evidence, make a bounded improvement, and explain the remaining risk.

## Required checks

### Content

- Plain-language instructions
- Explicit time estimate
- Stated prerequisites
- Core, build, and stretch paths
- Expected evidence
- Rubric with a pass rule
- Debrief and extension

### Technical

- Tool-agnostic baseline
- Version/date recorded for model or service
- Synthetic or approved data
- Reproducible setup
- Failure and fallback path
- No hidden dependency on privileged accounts

### Security and governance

- Threats and misuse are discussed at the correct level
- No unauthorized scanning, exploitation, exfiltration, or disruption
- Human approval is required for consequential actions
- Logs and evidence do not contain secrets
- Residual risk is documented

### Accessibility

- Headings are logical
- Tables have simple structure
- Instructions do not depend only on color, audio, or speed
- A low-bandwidth path exists
- Learners can submit plain-text evidence
- Trainer can offer equivalent non-code activities

## Evidence standard

A claim such as “the system is reliable” is not sufficient. The learner must show the test cases, results, decision rule, observed limitations, and next action.
