# Governance

## Purpose

This document explains how the open curriculum is maintained, reviewed, released, and improved.

## Roles

### Project steward

The project steward maintains the mission, licensing, safety boundaries, and overall direction.

### Curriculum maintainers

Curriculum maintainers review learning objectives, module quality, accessibility, technical accuracy, and assessment alignment.

### Trainers

Trainers pilot materials with learners, document friction, and propose improvements based on observed evidence.

### Contributors

Contributors may improve content, labs, templates, examples, documentation, or tooling by following CONTRIBUTING.md.

## Decision principles

Decisions should prioritize:

1. Learner safety
2. Practical learner capability
3. Evidence and reproducibility
4. Accessibility and plain language
5. Vendor neutrality
6. Responsible AI and cybersecurity practice
7. Sustainability for small organizations and nonprofits

## Change process

1. Open an issue describing the proposed change.
2. Identify the affected learner capability and module.
3. Explain safety, licensing, and assessment implications.
4. Submit a focused pull request.
5. Obtain review from a maintainer or designated trainer.
6. Update the changelog when the change affects a released module.

## Release levels

- Draft — idea or incomplete material
- Pilot — ready for supervised testing
- Reviewed — passed content, safety, and trainer review
- Stable — approved for general cohort use
- Retired — no longer recommended but preserved for history

## Assessment integrity

Public learning materials may be open. Confidential learner submissions, personal information, and unreleased assessment banks must not be committed to this repository.
