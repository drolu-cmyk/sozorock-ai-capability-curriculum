# M09 learner mission: AI Governance and Risk

## Mission

Practice how to describe an AI system across its lifecycle. Practice how to identify stakeholders, impacts, and accountability. Practice how to assess and prioritize AI risks. Practice how to define review, approval, monitoring, and retirement decisions.

## Time and prerequisites

- Time: 180 minutes
- Prerequisite: M08
- Work in the approved lab workspace.
- Use public, synthetic, or approved de-identified data only.

## Safety first

Do not test an unauthorized system, scan, exploit, exfiltrate, or disrupt anything external. Do not place secrets, credentials, personal information, protected health information, or confidential client material in the lab or portfolio. Stop before any irreversible action and ask for human approval.

## Steps

1. Choose the system from your capstone direction or use the fictional service-request workflow.
2. Complete the AI system inventory: purpose, users, owner, provider, model, data, geography, decisions affected, tools, and lifecycle stage.
3. Identify affected stakeholders and possible benefits, harms, exclusion, accessibility, privacy, security, and accountability concerns.
4. Define risk criteria using likelihood, impact, uncertainty, reversibility, and exposure.
5. Record at least six risks with owner, current control, residual risk, treatment, and decision date.
6. Define approval gates for design, pilot, deployment, material change, incident, and retirement.
7. Write the monitoring and withdrawal triggers.
8. Submit the inventory, impact map, risk register, and lifecycle review plan.

## Required submission

Submit:

- AI-system-inventory and risk-register
- the test cases and observed results
- the tool/model/version and date, if a tool was used
- the data and source boundary
- the limitation or residual risk
- the human-review or approval boundary

## Pathways

- Core: complete the task with the repository templates and a manual fallback.
- Build: use an approved low-code, API, or code implementation.
- Stretch: add a stronger evaluation, automation, or governance improvement without widening the safety boundary.

## Success check

You are finished when another person can understand what you built, reproduce the stated test, see where a human must decide, and identify what remains uncertain.
