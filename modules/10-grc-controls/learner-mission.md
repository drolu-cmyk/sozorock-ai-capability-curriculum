# M10 learner mission: GRC Implementation

## Mission

Practice how to translate AI risks into testable controls. Practice how to map controls to recognized frameworks. Practice how to define evidence, owners, frequency, and exceptions. Practice how to perform a small control test and report the result.

## Time and prerequisites

- Time: 180 minutes
- Prerequisite: M09
- Work in the approved lab workspace.
- Use public, synthetic, or approved de-identified data only.

## Safety first

Do not test an unauthorized system, scan, exploit, exfiltrate, or disrupt anything external. Do not place secrets, credentials, personal information, protected health information, or confidential client material in the lab or portfolio. Stop before any irreversible action and ask for human approval.

## Steps

1. Select five risks from M09 and write the control objective for each.
2. Create a control matrix with control statement, owner, population, frequency, evidence, test method, status, exception, and remediation.
3. Map each control to NIST AI RMF, NIST CSF 2.0, or ISO/IEC 42001 without implying certification.
4. Define evidence that a reviewer could inspect: inventory record, approval, test result, log, training record, incident record, or review note.
5. Test one preventive and one detective control using synthetic data or a mock workflow.
6. Record the test procedure, sample, result, limitation, and management action.
7. Write a one-page summary for a nontechnical decision-maker.
8. Submit the control matrix, evidence plan, test records, and management summary.

## Required submission

Submit:

- control-matrix and evidence-plan
- the test cases and observed results
- the tool/model/version and date, if a tool was used
- the data and source boundary
- the limitation or residual risk
- the human-review or approval boundary

## Pathways

- Core: complete the task with the repository templates and a manual fallback.
- Build: use an approved low-code, API, or code implementation.
- Stretch: add a stronger evaluation, automation, or governance improvement without widening the safety boundary.

## Success check

You are finished when another person can understand what you built, reproduce the stated test, see where a human must decide, and identify what remains uncertain.
