# M12 learner mission: Capstone Defense

## Mission

Practice how to explain the system and its evidence to a mixed audience. Practice how to defend scope, assumptions, controls, and limitations. Practice how to respond to challenge questions without overstating certainty. Practice how to prioritize the next improvement or decision.

## Time and prerequisites

- Time: 120 minutes
- Prerequisite: M11
- Work in the approved lab workspace.
- Use public, synthetic, or approved de-identified data only.

## Safety first

Do not test an unauthorized system, scan, exploit, exfiltrate, or disrupt anything external. Do not place secrets, credentials, personal information, protected health information, or confidential client material in the lab or portfolio. Stop before any irreversible action and ask for human approval.

## Steps

1. Prepare a ten-minute briefing: user problem, system boundary, value, evidence, threat model, controls, and residual risk.
2. Prepare a five-minute demonstration using synthetic or approved data only.
3. State what the system does not do and what remains human-owned.
4. Invite challenge questions about accuracy, data, security, privacy, bias, cost, accessibility, and failure.
5. Answer using evidence, uncertainty, or a clear commitment to investigate; do not invent facts or reveal hidden model reasoning.
6. Record each challenge, response, evidence reference, and unresolved issue.
7. Prioritize three improvements using impact, effort, and risk reduction.
8. Submit the briefing, demonstration record, challenge log, and improvement plan.

## Required submission

Submit:

- executive-briefing and improvement-plan
- the test cases and observed results
- the tool/model/version and date, if a tool was used
- the data and source boundary
- the limitation or residual risk
- the human-review or approval boundary

## Pathways

- Core: complete the task with the repository templates and a manual fallback.
- Build: use an approved low-code, API, or code implementation.
- Stretch: add a stronger evaluation, automation, or governance improvement without widening the safety boundary.

## Success check

You are finished when another person can understand what you built, reproduce the stated test, see where a human must decide, and identify what remains uncertain.
