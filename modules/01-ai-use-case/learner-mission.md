# Mission 01 — Frame a Safe AI Use Case

## Mission goal

Choose one real, low-risk workflow and determine whether AI should be used, how it could help, and what controls would be needed.

## Why this matters

A weak AI project begins with a tool. A strong AI project begins with a real problem, a measurable outcome, a known user, and an honest understanding of risk.

## Time

120 minutes.

## You will produce

- An AI use-case canvas
- A five-case test set
- A go/no-go recommendation

## Safety boundary

Use only synthetic, public, or de-identified information. Do not use real names, health records, financial records, credentials, confidential client information, or employment decisions.

## Step 1 — Choose a workflow

Choose a workflow that:

- Happens repeatedly
- Has a clear user
- Uses information you are allowed to handle
- Can be tested safely
- Does not make an irreversible decision about a person

Examples:

- Summarize public meeting notes
- Draft a first version of a community resource guide
- Classify incoming non-sensitive requests
- Turn a public policy document into questions and answers
- Create a first draft of a small-business workflow

Write the workflow in one sentence.

Checkpoint: another person should understand the workflow without asking what you mean.

## Step 2 — Describe the current process

Write five to eight steps showing how the work is completed today.

Record:

- Who performs each step
- What information they need
- Where delays occur
- Where errors occur
- What a successful result looks like

Do not assume AI is the answer. First understand the baseline.

## Step 3 — Identify the user and affected people

Record:

- Primary user
- Secondary users
- People affected by the output
- Person accountable for the final decision
- Person who can correct an error

Checkpoint: if no human is accountable, stop and redesign the workflow.

## Step 4 — Identify the data

List every type of information the workflow uses.

For each type, mark it:

- Public
- Internal
- Confidential
- Personal
- Highly sensitive

If the workflow requires highly sensitive information, choose a safer version for this mission.

## Step 5 — Define the AI task

Describe exactly what the AI would do.

Use a verb:

- Summarize
- Extract
- Classify
- Compare
- Draft
- Recommend
- Translate
- Retrieve

Avoid vague descriptions such as “use AI to improve the process.”

## Step 6 — Define the non-AI alternative

Describe the simplest non-AI approach that could solve the problem.

This becomes the baseline for comparing:

- Time
- Cost
- Quality
- Risk
- Accessibility
- Human workload

## Step 7 — Define success

Write three to five measurable success criteria.

Examples:

- At least 4 of 5 answers are supported by the source documents.
- A trained user completes the task 30% faster.
- The system never sends an external message without approval.
- All outputs identify uncertainty or missing information.
- No sensitive data leaves the approved workspace.

## Step 8 — Create five test cases

Write five realistic examples:

- One normal case
- One incomplete case
- One ambiguous case
- One difficult case
- One unsafe or out-of-scope case

Do not use real personal data.

## Step 9 — Identify initial risks

Record at least five risks. Consider:

- Incorrect output
- Unsupported claims
- Sensitive-data exposure
- Bias or unequal impact
- Unauthorized action
- Overreliance
- Vendor or supply-chain dependency
- Cost or availability failure
- Lack of human appeal or correction

For each risk, write one possible control.

## Step 10 — Make the recommendation

Choose one:

- Proceed to prototype
- Proceed with conditions
- Do not proceed
- Redesign the use case

Explain the decision in five sentences or fewer.

## Submission checklist

Submit:

- Completed AI use-case canvas
- Five-case test set
- Initial risk list
- Go/no-go recommendation
- One paragraph explaining what changed in your thinking

## Extension challenge

Create two alternative versions of the workflow:

1. A lower-risk version using only public information.
2. A higher-value version that would require stronger controls.

Explain which version should be prototyped first.
