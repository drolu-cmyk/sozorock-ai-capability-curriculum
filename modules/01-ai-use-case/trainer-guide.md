# Trainer Guide — Mission 01

## Trainer objective

Help learners move from “I want to use AI” to a specific, testable, low-risk use case with a clear human owner and measurable success criteria.

## Preparation checklist

- Review the learner mission and rubric.
- Prepare two safe example workflows.
- Prepare one deliberately unsafe example.
- Confirm that the workspace contains no production data.
- Confirm the submission location.
- Prepare a visible timer and a parking-lot list for questions outside scope.

## Suggested timing

- 10 minutes: frame the mission
- 10 minutes: demonstrate a weak and strong use-case statement
- 45 minutes: learner work
- 20 minutes: risk and test-case review
- 20 minutes: peer review
- 15 minutes: debrief and assessment

## Demonstration guidance

Show the difference between:

Weak: “Use AI to improve our organization.”

Strong: “Draft a first version of a public community-resource response from an approved source list, with citations and human review before publication.”

Do not build the learner's project for them.

## Coaching prompts

Use these questions:

- What happens today?
- Who is accountable for the final outcome?
- What would happen if the output were wrong?
- What data does the system actually need?
- What is the non-AI baseline?
- How will you know the system helped?
- What would make you stop the project?
- Which action must remain human-approved?

## Common failure modes

### Tool-first thinking

Ask the learner to close the tool and describe the workflow without mentioning AI.

### Vague use case

Require a specific user, task, input, output, and success measure.

### No human owner

Stop the exercise and require the learner to name the accountable person.

### Hidden sensitive data

Ask the learner to redesign the case using public, synthetic, or de-identified information.

### No baseline

Ask how the work is done today and what will be compared.

## Safety escalation

Pause the learner and notify the program lead if the learner attempts to:

- Upload real personal or protected data
- Use production credentials
- Automate an irreversible or high-impact decision
- Send external messages without approval
- Bypass access controls
- Test against a system they do not own or have permission to assess

## Debrief questions

- What did you initially assume AI should do?
- What did the baseline reveal?
- Which risk changed your design?
- What would you need to learn before prototyping?
- What evidence would convince a skeptical manager?

## Assessment

Use the Module 01 rubric. A learner may continue only when the canvas contains a defined user, accountable human owner, data classification, baseline, measurable success criteria, and initial controls.
